# Itemize launch product boundary

## Product promise

Itemize is a calm operating workspace for independent service businesses. It replaces the patchwork of tools needed to organize client work, move it forward, get approval, and get paid.

The product is not positioned as a collection of miniature SaaS clones. Individual tools earn their place by supporting one connected client-work lifecycle.

## Launch customer

The first customer is an independent service professional who currently combines notes or lists with several narrowly focused client tools. They value control and simplicity more than deep enterprise customization.

Studio serves the same customer after they add collaborators. Studio is not a separate agency-management product.

## Golden path

1. Create or import the first client.
2. Capture the work and next action in the workspace or pipeline.
3. Create the commercial artifact the work needs: estimate, invoice, or signature document.
4. Send it and observe its delivery or completion state.
5. Return to Itemize to see what needs attention next.

The primary activation event is not account creation. It is sending the first client-facing artifact to a real recipient. Workspace creation and adding the first client are leading indicators.

## Plan contract

| Plan | Product contract | Server access |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Free | Personal workspace: canvas, lists, notes, whiteboards, wireframes, sharing, settings | Workspace and account-management surfaces only |
| Solo | The complete independent-business lifecycle | Active subscription or unexpired Solo trial required for CRM, scheduling, campaigns, forms/pages, communication, reputation, invoicing, and signatures |
| Studio | Solo plus collaborators, higher limits, and connected social channels | Active subscription or unexpired Studio trial; social requires Studio or higher |
| Studio+ | Legacy tier for existing customers | Retained for compatibility; not promoted to new customers |

An expired trial, canceled subscription, unpaid subscription, or paid plan label without live billing grants no paid entitlement. Public recipient workflows, such as signing an already-sent document, remain available so customer obligations can be completed.

## Information architecture priority

The paid application should progressively converge on five user-facing groups:

1. Workspace
2. Clients
3. Documents and money
4. Scheduling and communication
5. Growth and automation

Campaigns, pages, forms, reputation, social, and advanced automation are expansion capabilities. They should not compete with the golden path during first-run onboarding.

## Onboarding contract

- Free signup lands in the workspace and does not expose the full business-tool navigation.
- Trial signup lands in the paid experience and is guided toward a real client-facing send.
- Empty states should offer one next action, use the language of the golden path, and avoid presenting every module at once.
- Upgrade prompts must preserve access to Free workspace data and state the business outcome being unlocked.
- Trial expiry must never imply that workspace data was deleted.

## Metrics for the first commercial release

- Time from signup to first workspace item
- Time from trial signup to first client
- Time from first client to first estimate, invoice, or signature send
- Percentage of trials that send a real client-facing artifact
- Trial-to-paid conversion after that send
- Weekly return rate to view or advance an outstanding client item
- Number of external subscriptions customers report replacing

Feature usage alone is not a launch success metric. A feature matters when it shortens or completes the client-work lifecycle.

## Launch exclusions

- Do not market Studio+ to new customers.
- Do not lead onboarding with white-label, SaaS-resale, API, or advanced automation concepts.
- Do not add a new top-level module unless it clearly strengthens the golden path or demonstrates paid retention.
- Do not treat a frontend lock as authorization; paid access must be enforced by the server.
